# 00 — Start here

## The solo version

Two versions of this kit exist. This is the one for a business run by one
person. If more than one of you works in it and uses Claude, use
`shared-business-brain` instead — it asks different questions and has one
extra sitting this version does not need.

The versions differ by how many people are in the business. Not by what you
pay for Claude. This kit works on Free or Pro, and moves with you if you
change plan or take on your first employee — the routes and the migration
notes are in `03-load-the-brain`.

## What this fixes

Claude opens every conversation knowing nothing about your business. It does
not know what you sell, how you sound, or what you decided last month. So
every chat starts with the same ten minutes of re-explaining.

This kit fixes that with eight short files Claude reads before it answers. No
software, nothing to maintain.

About ninety minutes, spread over a week or two. Read this page, then open
`01-interviews`.

## The second reason to do it

Everything about your business currently lives in your head, and Claude's
memory of it is partial and unevenly reliable. These files are the version
you can read, check, correct and hand to someone.

The first person you hire, the bookkeeper, whoever covers while you are ill —
they do not need your memory. They need `how-i-work.md` and
`customer-faq.md`. Hand those over and someone can start being useful the
same day. That is worth more than the time this saves you in chats, and
`03-load-the-brain/03-sharing-the-brain.md` suggests who gets which files.

## What is in here

| Folder | What it is | When |
| --- | --- | --- |

| `02-brain-files` | The eight files, blank | Attached to the interviews |

## How the interviews work

One sitting is one file, one chat. Start a new chat, attach the file named at
the top of the sitting, paste the prompt, answer the questions. At the end
Claude writes the finished file. Save it, close the chat.

Short chats are the point. In a long conversation Claude starts cutting
corners — batching questions, accepting vague answers, eventually writing
answers for you to nod at. Ten minutes at a time keeps it sharp, and lets you
stop without losing your place.

## The sittings

| # | About | Time | Have open |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 00 | What Claude already knows about you | 10 min | — |
| 01 | The business itself | 12 min | — |
| 02 | What you sell | 15 min | Prices, website or booking system |
| 03 | How you work, and what only you know | 12 min | — |
| 04 | Customer questions | 15 min | Inbox or messages |
| 05 | Pricing and offers | 12 min | — |
| 06 | Clients and suppliers | 10 min | — |
| 07 | Decisions already made | 10 min | — |
| 08 | How you sound — the rules | 8 min | — |
| 09 | How you sound — the examples | 12 min | Your published writing |

One a day for a fortnight is a perfectly good pace.

## Order

**00 first, 01 second.** Sitting 00 shows you what Claude already holds.
Sitting 01 establishes what the business is, and ends by telling you which
later sittings need adapting for your kind of work — a service business
reframes sitting 02 around rates and availability rather than stock. Note
what it says before carrying on.

**08 before 09.** They build the same file in two passes. For sitting 09,
attach the file produced by 08, not the blank one.

The rest can happen in any order.

## Each sitting, step by step

1. Start a **new chat** — not a continuation of an earlier one.
2. Attach the file named at the top of that sitting, from `02-brain-files`.
3. Paste everything below the line in the prompt.
4. Answer the questions.
5. Copy the finished file out. **Keep the same file name** — the instructions
   and the skills look files up by name.
6. Save it over the blank one.
7. Close the chat.

If you get interrupted, start a new chat, attach the part-finished file
instead of the blank one, and paste the same prompt. Claude picks up at the
first gap.

## Three rules for you

**Do not invent an answer to keep things moving.** Say you do not know, and
it gets marked as an open decision. A marked hole gets filled later. A
made-up answer becomes something you then work from.

**Push back.** If Claude writes a sentence you would not say, tell it. Do not
accept a voice that is not yours because it reads well.

**Sitting 03 matters most,** and it is the easiest to answer quickly. The
question about what only you know is the whole reason that file exists.

## When the sittings are done

Open `03-load-the-brain`.

One thing to watch: if you already run a Project with these standing
instructions, do the interviews **outside** it. Those instructions tell
Claude to check past decisions and end by logging one — useful later,
distracting during an interview.

## What this will not do

It does not read your email overnight or learn on its own. Tools that do need
someone technical to run them, and at your size the upkeep costs more than
the insight.

And it only works if you write to it. After any conversation that settles
something, ask Claude to write the line for `decisions.md` and paste it in.
Two minutes. That is the whole loop — everything else here is scaffolding
around it.
