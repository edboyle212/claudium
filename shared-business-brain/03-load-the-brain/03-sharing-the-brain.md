# Sharing the brain

## Share it with whoever you want

These are your files. Nothing here restricts who can have them, and there is
no reason to keep them to the people who built them.

Worth sending to:

- a contractor, freelancer or VA doing work for you
- a new starter, on day one, instead of a fortnight of questions
- whoever covers while you are away
- your accountant, bookkeeper or agency
- a partner business that has to describe what you sell
- someone using a different AI tool entirely — these are plain text files and
  work anywhere

Send the files, or a link to the folder. Both are fine.

## Choose which files, not whether to share

The only judgement worth making is *which* of the eight go to a given person.
Some hold things you may not want in general circulation:

- `offer-rules.md` — your floor price and your discount reasoning
- `relationships.md` — client concentration, supplier weak points, who a
  relationship really sits with
- `how-i-work.md` or `channels.md` — what breaks when you are away
- `decisions.md` — the reasoning behind choices, including ones you reversed

Nothing stops you sending those. Just decide, rather than attaching all eight
by reflex.

## Share sets that work

| Who | Send them |

| Someone answering customers | `customer-faq.md`, `offerings.md`, `voice.md` |
| A new starter, week one | `business.md`, `offerings.md`, `customer-faq.md`, `voice.md` |
| A new starter, once settled | all eight |
| Accountant or bookkeeper | `offerings.md`, `offer-rules.md` |
| Someone covering while you are away | all eight, and say so out loud |

Adjust freely. This table is a starting point, not a policy.

## One person keeps the master current

This is about maintenance, not permission. Somebody has to be the one who
holds the copy everyone else copies from — otherwise two people edit two
versions and neither knows which is right.

That person does not approve who gets the files. Anyone can share them with
anyone. All the owner does is keep one copy correct and say when it changes.

## A copy you send is a copy

It will not update when you update yours. So:

- put the date in the message when you send it
- if you have shared the folder rather than the files, say so — that copy
  stays current on its own
- when something important changes, tell whoever is working from an old copy

For anyone regularly working from the files, share the folder rather than the
files. It saves the whole problem.

## What not to put in these files in the first place

The interviews are written to keep this out already, but it is worth knowing
why: nothing here should hold individual customer records, order histories,
personal contact details, passwords or account credentials. Those live in the
systems built for them.

That is what makes these files easy to share without thinking hard each time.
